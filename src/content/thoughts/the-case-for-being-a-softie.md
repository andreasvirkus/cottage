I have a confession: I'm a pushover. Well - that's the word my brain reaches for on the bad days.
On the good days I'd call it something nicer, like generous, or accommodating, or just... a softie.

Most of what raised me, and most of what my career or media has tried to teach me since, points the other
way. Negotiate the raise! Protect your time! Say no more often! Don't be the one who always
takes the notes, covers the shift, picks up the ticket nobody wanted. Advocate for yourself,
loudly and often, or the world will let you disappear into everyone else's priorities.

It's good advice! I've taken plenty of it (well, some...), and I guess I'm better off for it. But it's also advice built
for a specific kind of person, and somewhere along the way it started getting handed out as if it
were the *only* correct way to be wired. As if every person who bends over backwards for others
is a self-advocacy problem waiting to be solved, rather than just... a different kind of person.

## two engines

Here's the thing I've come around to: some people are charged up by winning things for
themselves - be it a raise, promotion, or the credit. And some people
are charged up by being useful to someone else. Not out of fear, not because they don't know how
to say no, but because watching someone's shoulders drop with relief is, genuinely, fuel for them.

I'm solidly in the second camp. Give me the gnarly bug a teammate's been stuck on for two days
and let me be the one who finally cracks it with them, and I'll be in a good mood for the rest of
the week.

## the well and the puddle

But none of this is a free pass to pour yourself out until there's nothing left, and I don't want to
undersell that part. There's a clear difference between two people who both say yes to everything:
one is a well, one is a puddle.

A puddle gives because it's afraid of what happens if it doesn't - afraid of conflict, of being
seen as difficult, of taking up space. It has no source feeding it, so eventually it just
evaporates, and is left confused about where their energy went.

A well gives because it's connected to something underneath that keeps refilling it. It still has
edges - a well can run dry too, and a good one gets rested and topped up on purpose. The giving
is just water passing through on its way somewhere it's needed. That
upkeep and self-care part is very much needed & I'm not arguing my way out of that.

The difference isn't how much you give, but whether something's replenishing you while you do it.

## so, softness as a feature

What I want is for "I get a lot out of taking care of people" to be allowed to sit next to
"I know how to ask for what I need," rather than be treated as the thing that needs fixing before
the second one can happen. They're not opposites. Plenty of the most generous people I know are
also perfectly capable of telling you no - they just spend that capacity on the things worth
spending it on. I've also found this to be easier at companies where I as an employee feel taken
care of - so I have less of a need to fight for a raise or have those conversations that 
feel really difficult to me. The ones where you have to make it clear you don't feel valued.

Hustle culture will keep telling me to advocate harder for myself, and now and then, on the
things that matter, I will. But most days I'll happily go stretch a little thin for someone else,
because it turns out that's just how I'm charged.
